A Companion of the Prophet ﷺ

Nusaybah Bint Kaʿb (RA)

Umm ʿĀmmārah, the Lioness of Uḥud, who stood as a shield before the Messenger ﷺ

In 625 CE, when the Muslim lines broke at Uḥud, a woman of Madinah picked up a sword and stood between the Prophet ﷺ and his enemies.

Who Was Nusaybah?

Nusaybah bint Kaʿb al-Māziniyyah (RA) belonged to the Banu Najjār of the Ansar in Madinah, and is best known by her kunya, Umm ʿĀmmārah. She was among the early Muslims of Yathrib and lived through nearly every major turning point of the Prophet’s ﷺ Madinan years. Her husband Zayd ibn ʿĀṣim and her sons ʿAbdullah and Ḥabīb shared her faith and her sacrifices. History remembers her as a believer who treated the defence of the Messenger ﷺ as a duty, not an option.

Family and Lineage

Her full name was Nusaybah bint Kaʿb ibn ʿAmr ibn ʿAwf, of the Banū Māzin ibn al-Najjār, a clan of the Khazraj in Madinah. The Banū al-Najjār were the Prophet’s ﷺ maternal relatives through his great-grandmother Salmā, and he stayed among them when he first arrived in the city. She married Zayd ibn ʿĀṣim, and they had two sons, ʿAbdullah and Ḥabīb. After Zayd she married Ghaziyyah ibn ʿAmr and had more children. Her kunya, Umm ʿĀmmārah, comes from her son ʿĀmmārah. Her brothers are also named among the early Ansar, and the early biographers record that her whole household took the Prophet’s ﷺ side.

Early Life and Her Road to Islam

Nusaybah grew up in Yathrib in the years before the Hijrah, in a city divided between the Aws and the Khazraj and worn down by long feuds, most recently the Battle of Buʿāth (around 617 CE). Like the other households of the Banu al-Najjār, her family is understood to have embraced Islam through the teaching of Muṣʿab ibn ʿUmayr (RA), who lodged with Asʿad ibn Zurārah, a man of the same clan. When the Prophet ﷺ arrived in 622 CE, his camel came to rest in the Najjār quarter, and he stayed in the house of Abū Ayyūb al-Anṣārī (RA) while the mosque was built. She therefore lived in the very neighbourhood where the first Muslim community of Madinah took shape.

The Pledge of ʿAqabah

Nusaybah is counted among the two women who travelled from Madinah to Mecca for the second pledge of ʿAqabah, where the Ansar swore to protect the Prophet ﷺ as they would protect their own families. She had made that promise with her own voice, and she would keep it with her own sword.

The pledge took place in the valley of Mina during the pilgrimage season, one year before the Hijrah. About seventy-three men and two women from Yathrib crept out of their camp by night, slipped past the Quraysh, and met the Prophet ﷺ in secret, with his uncle al-ʿAbbās present. The two women were Nusaybah and Asmāʾ bint ʿAmr. Both gave the same pledge to shelter the Messenger ﷺ at the cost of their own safety, in a city that was about to go to war to protect him.

QURʾĀN, SŪRAH AL-MUMTAḤANAH 60:12 (MEANING)

“O Prophet, when believing women come to you pledging that they will not associate anything with Allah… then accept their pledge and ask Allah to forgive them.”

ʿĀʾishah (RA) described how the Prophet ﷺ took the pledge from women: he did it by spoken words alone, and his hand never touched the hand of any woman who was not his own. This is recorded in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī. Nusaybah and Asmāʾ therefore pledged by their word, and they were remembered for it.

Her Courage at Uḥud

She first came to the field to carry water and tend the wounded. When the archers left their post and enemy cavalry broke through, the battle turned and the Prophet ﷺ was left with only a few defenders. Nusaybah put down the water skin, took up a sword and shield, and moved to his side. Her conduct that day is remembered in four moments:

  1. Wielding a sword. She fought off approaching enemies and struck at riders who charged toward the Prophet ﷺ, defending him with sword and bow.
  2. Fierce courage. The Prophet ﷺ later recounted that wherever he turned, left or right, on the day of Uḥud, he saw her fighting in front of him.
  3. Enduring wounds. She was left with some twelve or thirteen wounds, depending on the report. When her son was wounded mid-battle she bandaged him and told him to go back and defend the Messenger ﷺ.
  4. The ultimate reward. Once she was able to speak, she asked after the Prophet’s ﷺ safety. Moved by her devotion, he prayed that she and her family would be his companions in Paradise.

The battle took place in Shawwāl, 3 AH (March 625 CE). A Qurayshī army of about three thousand marched on Madinah to avenge Badr. The Prophet ﷺ placed fifty archers on the hill behind the Muslim lines and ordered them not to leave it whatever happened. When the Muslims seemed to have won, most of the archers left their post for the spoils. Khālid ibn al-Walīd led the cavalry around the hill and struck the Muslims from behind, and the army scattered. The Qurʾān speaks of this moment directly in Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:152–153.

Nusaybah had gone out to the field with a water skin, as many Madinan women did. When the Muslims broke, she saw the Prophet ﷺ standing with only a few men around him. She dropped the skin, took up a sword and shield, and moved forward. The early biographers (al-Wāqidī, Ibn Saʿd) report that she fought the enemy’s horsemen on foot and shot arrows with a bow, until she had fought her way to the Messenger’s ﷺ side.

The enemy fighter ʿAmr ibn Qamiʾah (known as Ibn Qamiʾah) rode at the Prophet ﷺ, and Nusaybah met him. He struck her on the shoulder, leaving a deep wound that reports say needed a year to heal. She struck back at him with her sword, but he wore two coats of armour and survived. Her son ʿAbdullah ibn Zayd was wounded in the arm, and she bound the wound with a cloth she carried and said, “Get up, my son, and fight the people.” The Prophet ﷺ is reported to have said, “Who can bear what you bear, Umm ʿĀmmārah?”. When her son struck down the man who had wounded her, the Prophet ﷺ smiled and said that Allah had given her what she wanted.

A ṣaḥīḥ report gives the context for her stand. Anas (RA) said that when the Muslims scattered, the Prophet ﷺ was left with seven of the Ansar and two of Quraysh. He asked who would drive the enemy off, promising that man Paradise or that he would be his companion in Paradise. One Ansārī stepped forward and was killed, then another, until all seven had fallen (Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, Kitāb al-Jihād). Nusaybah’s stand belongs to this same defence.

She did not stand alone. The early biographers report that her husband Zayd and both of her sons, ʿAbdullah and Ḥabīb, fought at Uḥud as well, so a whole household was defending the Prophet ﷺ that day. He is reported to have prayed for the family, asking Allah to bless them. Around them, others held the same line: Abū Ṭalḥah (RA) stood in front of the Prophet ﷺ with his shield and shot arrows over him, and Anas reports in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī that Abū Ṭalḥah broke two or three bows that day (Kitāb al-Jihād).

The fighting did not end her service. Reports say that on the next morning, when the Prophet ﷺ marched out to Ḥamrāʾ al-Asad to pursue the Quraysh, she tied her waist-wrapper to go with the army but could not, because her wound had drained her strength. After he returned he asked after her and was glad to hear she was alive. Her shoulder wound took about a year to heal.

What the Prophet ﷺ Said

As she stood beside her wounded son, the Prophet ﷺ is reported to have marvelled at her steadiness, saying that no one could bear what she was bearing. She asked him to pray that she and her family might be his companions in Paradise, and he made that prayer for them. Her reply is among the most quoted lines in the Seerah.

REPORTED IN THE EARLY SOURCES OF THE SEERAH

“Wherever I turned, left or right, on the Day of Uḥud, I saw her fighting for me.”

REPORTED BY IBN HISHĀM, AL-WĀQIDĪ AND IBN SAʿD

Umm ʿĀmmārah said: “Messenger of Allah, ask Allah that we be your companions in Paradise.” He said: “O Allah, make them my companions in Paradise.” She replied: “I no longer care what befalls me in this world.”

Her request echoes a promise in the Qurʾān. Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:69 says that whoever obeys Allah and the Messenger will be with those Allah has blessed: the prophets, the truthful, the martyrs and the righteous, and what excellent companions they are. The Prophet ﷺ also said, in a ḥadīth narrated by Anas in Bukhārī and Muslim, that a person will be with those he loves.

A note on sources. The Qurʾānic verses and the ṣaḥīḥ ḥadīths on this page give the context of Uḥud. The detailed accounts of Nusaybah’s own deeds come from the early sīrah and biographical works. The details of those works differ in places, so wound counts and quotations are given as “reported”.

Hudaybiyyah, Ḥunayn and Yamāmah

Uḥud was not her only battlefield. She is reported to have taken part in the pledge of Riḍwān at Ḥudaybiyyah, in Ḥunayn, and in the campaigns after the Prophet’s ﷺ passing. At the Battle of Yamāmah against the false prophet Musaylimah, she joined the army to avenge her son Ḥabīb, who had been killed by him. She fought until she was badly wounded and lost a hand, and returned to Madinah with some dozen wounds on her body.

The Qurʾān records the pledge of Ḥudaybiyyah in Sūrah al-Fatḥ 48:18, where Allah says He was pleased with the believers who pledged to the Prophet ﷺ under the tree. Nusaybah is listed among those present. Her later campaigns were Khaybar, the ʿUmrah of fulfilment and Ḥunayn, where the Muslims were first scattered and then rallied.

Her son Ḥabīb ibn Zayd had been sent as an envoy to Musaylimah. He refused to say that Musaylimah was a prophet, and Musaylimah had him cut to pieces. When Abū Bakr (RA) gathered an army against Musaylimah, Nusaybah asked to go. Reports say Abū Bakr, who knew her record, did not refuse her. At Yamāmah she fought through the enemy ranks towards Musaylimah. She lost a hand and received eleven more wounds, and her son ʿAbdullah is reported to have taken part in killing Musaylimah. After the battle she returned to Madinah and lived on. The exact year of her death is not firmly recorded.

Ḥunayn (8 AH). After the conquest of Makkah, about twelve thousand Muslims marched against the tribes of Hawāzin and Thaqīf. The enemy archers ambushed them in a narrow valley, and many of the Muslims scattered. The Prophet ﷺ held his ground with a small group, and al-Barāʾ ibn ʿĀzib (RA) describes in Bukhārī how he stayed on his mule and called out his name and lineage. Sūrah al-Tawbah 9:25–26 says that their large numbers did not help them, until Allah sent down His tranquillity upon the Messenger and the believers. Nusaybah is recorded among those present at Ḥunayn, who stood firm with the Prophet ﷺ.

Yamāmah (12 AH). The Battle of Yamāmah was fought in 633 CE under Khālid ibn al-Walīd (RA) against the Banū Ḥanīfah, who followed Musaylimah. The fighting was fierce, and the final stand took place in a walled orchard later called the Garden of Death. Musaylimah was killed there, reportedly by Waḥshī and an Anṣārī. So many Qurʾān reciters fell in this battle that ʿUmar (RA) urged Abū Bakr (RA) to have the Qurʾān gathered in one book. Zayd ibn Thābit (RA) tells this story in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī. Nusaybah lost her hand in this same battle.

A narrator of ḥadīth. Nusaybah is also counted among the women who transmitted ḥadīth. She reported that the Prophet ﷺ once visited her and she offered him food. When she said she was fasting, he told her that the angels send blessings on a fasting person while others eat in his presence. This is recorded by al-Tirmidhī, who graded it ḥasan, and by Ibn Mājah.

Her Contribution at a Glance

  1. Pledge-keeper. One of the two women at the second pledge of ʿAqabah, and faithful to it for the rest of her life.
  2. Defender of the Prophet ﷺ. At Uḥud she moved to his side when the army broke and fought to protect him, taking twelve or thirteen wounds.
  3. Nurse and water-bearer. She tended the wounded, including her own son, in the manner Bukhārī describes for the women of Uḥud.
  4. Mother of fighters. She raised sons who fought beside her and sent them back into battle.
  5. Veteran of many campaigns. Uḥud, Ḥudaybiyyah, Khaybar, ʿUmrat al-Qaḍāʾ, Ḥunayn and Yamāmah, where she lost a hand.
  6. Transmitter of knowledge. She passed on a ḥadīth about the angels’ blessing on the fasting.

Qurʾān and Ḥadīth Behind Her Story

Several verses and ṣaḥīḥ reports explain what Nusaybah stood for. The verses are given in meaning, and the ḥadīths are cited by collection.

  1. Āl ʿImrān 3:139. “Do not lose heart nor grieve, for you will be the higher if you are believers.” This was revealed after the defeat at Uḥud, to steady the survivors.
  2. Āl ʿImrān 3:144. Muḥammad ﷺ is only a Messenger, and other messengers passed before him. If he dies or is killed, the believers must not turn back on their heels. This answers the rumour of his death at Uḥud.
  3. Āl ʿImrān 3:195. Allah says He will never let the work of any worker among the believers be lost, whether man or woman, for they are from one another. Scholars often cite this verse for women who took part in the early struggles.
  4. Āl ʿImrān 3:169–171. Those killed in Allah’s way are alive with their Lord and given provision. The verses were revealed about the martyrs of Uḥud.
  5. al-Tawbah 9:71. Believing men and women are allies of one another: they command what is right, forbid what is wrong, pray and give zakāh.
  6. al-Aḥzāb 33:35. Allah lists the believing men and women together, and prepares for them forgiveness and a great reward.
  7. Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī, Kitāb al-Jihād. Anas (RA) saw ʿĀʾishah and Umm Sulaym at Uḥud carrying water skins on their backs and pouring water into the mouths of the wounded.
  8. Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī, Kitāb al-Jihād. Al-Rubayyiʿ bint Muʿawwidh (RA) said that the women went out with the Prophet ﷺ to give water, nurse the wounded and bring the dead and injured back to Madinah.
  9. Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī. After Uḥud, Qays ibn Abī Ḥāzim saw the hand of Ṭalḥah ibn ʿUbaydillāh paralysed, because it had shielded the Prophet ﷺ in that battle.

Lessons from Her Life

  1. A promise kept. She gave her word at ʿAqabah and honoured it for the rest of her life, at Uḥud, Ḥunayn and Yamāmah.
  2. Care and courage together. She nursed her son and then sent him back to the fight, doing both with the same steadiness.
  3. The goal was the Hereafter. Her request was to be with the Prophet ﷺ in Paradise, and everything else was secondary to that.
  4. A place for women in the early community. The sources show women nursing, giving water and, when the situation demanded it, defending the Prophet ﷺ.
  5. Loss without giving up. She lost her son Ḥabīb to Musaylimah and still rode to Yamāmah.
Nusaybah bint Kaʿb (RA) is remembered as a mother, a nurse, a pledge-keeper and a warrior, and above all as a woman who gave everything she had to defend the Messenger of Allah ﷺ.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Who was Nusaybah bint Kaʿb (RA)?

She was a companion of the Prophet ﷺ from the Ansar of Madinah, known as Umm ʿĀmmārah. She pledged allegiance at ʿAqabah and fought to defend the Prophet ﷺ at Uḥud.

2. Why is she called the Lioness of Uḥud?

At Uḥud she fought on the Prophet’s ﷺ left and right when the Muslim lines were breached, and he later said he saw her fighting for him wherever he turned.

3. How many wounds did she suffer?

Reports differ. The early sources mention about twelve wounds, and some narrations give thirteen. All agree they were severe.

4. What did the Prophet ﷺ pray for her?

When she asked to be with him in Paradise, he prayed that she and her family would be his companions there.

5. Did she fight in other battles?

Yes. She took part in Ḥudaybiyyah and Ḥunayn, and at Yamāmah she lost a hand while fighting Musaylimah’s army.

6. What does “Umm ʿĀmmārah” mean?

It means “mother of ʿĀmmārah.” This kunya comes from her son ʿĀmmārah, and she was known by it throughout the early sources.

7. Was she the first woman to fight in Islam?

The early sources do not call her the first. Other women also took part in the early campaigns, such as Ṣafiyyah bint ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib, Umm Sulaym and al-Rubayyiʿ bint Muʿawwidh. Nusaybah is the one remembered above all for defending the Prophet ﷺ in person at Uḥud.

8. Is her story in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī or Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim?

The detailed account of her fighting at Uḥud comes mainly from Ibn Isḥāq and Ibn Hishām, al-Wāqidī and Ibn Saʿd. The Ṣaḥīḥayn confirm the wider context, that women fought and tended the wounded at Uḥud and that the Prophet ﷺ was left with only a few defenders.

9. Which Qurʾānic verses speak about Uḥud?

Sūrah Āl ʿImrān 3:121–179 deals with the battle. Key passages are 3:139 (do not lose heart), 3:144 (Muḥammad ﷺ is only a Messenger), 3:152–153 (the archers’ mistake) and 3:169–171 (the martyrs).

10. What happened to her son Ḥabīb?

The Prophet ﷺ sent Ḥabīb ibn Zayd as an envoy to Musaylimah. When he refused to call Musaylimah a prophet, he was killed. Nusaybah’s fight at Yamāmah was in part a response to this loss.

11. What was her wish when she asked for the Prophet’s ﷺ prayer?

She asked to be his companion in Paradise together with her family, and the Prophet ﷺ prayed for exactly that. Sūrah al-Nisāʾ 4:69 gives the same promise to those who obey Allah and the Messenger.

12. Who were the Ansar?

The Ansar (“helpers”) were the Muslims of Madinah, from the tribes of Aws and Khazraj, who welcomed the Prophet ﷺ and the emigrants from Mecca. Nusaybah belonged to the Khazraj.

13. How many women pledged at the second ʿAqabah?

Two women are named among the seventy-three men: Nusaybah bint Kaʿb and Asmāʾ bint ʿAmr, known as Umm Manīʿ.

14. What can we learn from her?

That faith is shown by action. She kept her pledge, cared for her family, stayed steady under injury and loss, and asked Allah for the highest reward. Many readers see the same resolve in the verses quoted above.
